# Design & Architecture Decisions — AcdyOn Pathway AI

## 1. Why this approach?

I chose the Premium Home Page track because I felt I could create more value by making the product itself understandable through the website rather than building a homepage that only talks about AcdyOn.

The main idea I explored was Pathway AI — a concept experience where a user can select their goal, current context, and priority and then receive a recommended learning direction. I chose this approach over a more traditional marketing homepage because it gives the visitor something to actually interact with. Instead of simply saying that AcdyOn provides personalized learning, the page lets the user experience the idea through a working interaction.

I also tried to keep the concept honest. Pathway AI is presented as a product concept for this submission rather than an existing AcdyOn product, and I avoided adding fabricated testimonials, student numbers, ratings, logos, or performance claims.

## 2. Trade-off

With the time limit, I decided to go deeper on the Pathway AI experience instead of building many additional sections or features.

I prioritized the complete flow — selecting a goal, understanding the user's context, selecting a priority, generating a recommendation, explaining why that direction was selected, and providing a next action. I used deterministic client-side logic rather than building a real AI/backend recommendation system because it was more reliable and easier to verify within the available time.

With a real week, I would connect the experience to a backend and make the recommendations and AcdyOn programme information data-driven. I would also spend more time on usability testing with different users and refine the recommendation logic based on their feedback.

## 3. AI usage

I used AI tools mainly as a development and design assistant during the implementation. I used them to explore UI ideas, improve the component structure, work through implementation details, and identify possible responsive and interaction issues.

I did not treat the generated output as final. I personally reviewed the implementation, changed the design decisions where needed, tested the interactions, checked the responsive layouts, verified the recommendation flow, and ran the production build.

The final product direction, visual choices, interaction flow, and trade-offs were decisions I made for the submission.
